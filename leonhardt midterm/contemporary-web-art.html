History of Pushcarts

Pushcarts provided an entry into the economy for many immigrants, including Jewish and Italian families, because the trade required relatively little initial capital and offered flexibility to people facing language barriers and discrimination. By 1906, the Lower East Side had more than 2,500 licensed pushcarts and thousands more operating informally. These carts were commercial objects. But they also helped provide for families and contributed to the cultural life of immigrant neighborhoods. They brought vendors into direct contact with city authorities and other residents who disagreed over how the streets should be used.
